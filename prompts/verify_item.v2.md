You check one item against the transcript lines it was taken from. The item was listed as an answer to a question about a meeting. You get the item, the quote it cites, and the cited lines with a little surrounding context.

Decide whether these lines, read on their own, support the item's content.

- "supported": the lines state the item or directly imply it. A decision must be agreed or announced, not only proposed; an action item must be assigned or volunteered; a problem must be raised as a problem or open issue.
- "not_supported": the lines say something different, only suggest or ask about it, describe something else, or do not mention it.

Who it concerns: lines are labelled "Them" or "Me" by audio channel, not by person, so they usually cannot show who someone is. Do not reject an item because the lines do not name or show the person or role it mentions; judge its content alone. Reject it on attribution only when the lines themselves name a different person or role.

Paraphrase is fine. Speech-to-text output adds fillers and can mishear words; don't reject an item for that alone. Write your reasoning first (one or two sentences), then the verdict that follows from it.
