## 2. Power: how many questions do we need?

Questions needed for an 80% chance of detecting a gap at alpha = 0.05 with a paired (McNemar) test. What matters is the **discordance**: the share of questions on which exactly one of the two systems is right. Systems that fail on the same hard questions disagree less, and then a smaller sample suffices.

| Pair | Discordance | n for 5 pts | n for 10 pts | Detectable gap @ 10 | @ 120 | @ 206 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| b0_topk vs b1_summaries | 20% | 626 | 155 | 62 pts | 11 pts | 9 pts |
| b2_full vs b0_topk | 10% | 312 | 77 | 62 pts | 8 pts | 6 pts |
| b0_topk vs b3_agentic | 20% | 626 | 155 | 62 pts | 11 pts | 9 pts |

Discordance estimated from a small pilot is itself noisy; a pair with 0 observed disagreements is floored at the gap size (the most optimistic case).

## 3. Cost vs accuracy (Pareto frontier)

A system is **dominated** if another is at least as accurate and at least as cheap (and, in the last column, at least as fast), and strictly better on one. Accuracy gaps here are point estimates; check §1 before calling a domination real.
